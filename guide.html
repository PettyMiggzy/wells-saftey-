About the passcode. Both private pages ask for a passcode. It keeps the pages away from anyone who stumbles onto the link — but it is not a real login, and anyone who knows their way around a browser could get past it. Do not treat it as a lock on a safe. What actually protects your numbers is that they live on your own machine, not on a website somebody could break into.

Change it in Settings → Passcode, and tell your drivers the new one. Changing it only affects the browser you change it in.

2. Getting paid

Set this up once

  1. Settings → Your business. Your name, address, phone, and how people pay you (check, Zelle, ACH). This prints at the top of every invoice.
  2. Settings → Default rates. Put your real numbers in: lead, chase, high pole, steer, route survey, deadhead, wait time, per diem, fuel. The ones in there now are placeholders — change them to what you actually charge.
  3. Clients → New Customer. Add the brokers you run for, with their payment terms (30 days, 45 days). Then you never retype them.

Every invoice after that

  1. Go to Clients, find the broker, hit Invoice. The due date fills in from their terms automatically.
  2. Fill in the load number, permit, and where it went.
  3. Click the buttons for what you ran — Lead escort, High pole, and so on. Each one drops a line in at your saved rate. Type the miles over the quantity.
  4. Set status to Sent / unpaid and hit Save invoice.
  5. Hit Print / PDF. On the print screen choose Save as PDF, then email it to the broker.

Chasing the money

The Dashboard is built around one question: who owes you and how late are they.

  • Outstanding — everything billed and not yet paid.
  • Overdue — past the due date. This is your call list.
  • Aging — how long it has been sitting. Anything in the 31–60 column or worse turns red, because that is when money starts going missing for good.
  • Who owes you — by broker, biggest first, with how many days late their oldest one is.

When a cheque lands, hit Mark paid on that row. That is the whole job — it dates it today and moves it out of your Outstanding.

For your bookkeeper or at tax time: Invoices → Export CSV. That opens in Excel and has every invoice, what it was for, and whether it was paid.

3. Your drivers

The Drivers tab is a roster, but the part worth using is the credential dates. Put in when each card expires — PEVO, ATSSA, WITPAC, TIMS, medical card, insurance.

Anything expiring inside 60 days, or already expired, jumps to the top of your Dashboard with the number of days on it. A lapsed card parks a driver, and finding out at a scale house is the expensive way to find out.

Set them up once for yourself, Tim and Cole, and the Dashboard nags you instead of you having to remember.

4. Job reports from the road

Send your drivers the driver.html link and the passcode. After a run they fill out four short screens on their phone: who they are, the run, photos, then send.

They can photograph the load, the permit, damage, receipts — whatever backs up the job. The photos get shrunk automatically so they actually send over a phone signal instead of failing.

How it reaches you

Right now, when they hit Send to dispatch, their phone opens its normal share menu with the report and photos attached, and they text or email it to you. You save the attachment, then in the Dispatch Book go to Reports → Import a report and pick it.

Importing the same one twice is harmless — it notices and skips it.

The good bit. Open an imported report and hit Make an invoice from this. It prices the whole run off your saved rates — the miles for each thing they ran, plus deadhead, wait time and per diem — and picks the customer if you already have them. A 150 mile lead and high pole run with 40 deadhead, 2 hours waiting and a night out comes out at $1,082.50 without you typing a number. Check it over, then send it.

Once a report has been invoiced it gets marked, so you cannot accidentally bill the same run twice.

5. Not losing everything

Read this bit twice. Right now everything — invoices, customers, drivers — is saved in the browser on that one computer. Nothing is on the internet. That is private and it is free, but it means:
  • If you clear your browsing history the wrong way, it is gone.
  • Your phone and your laptop keep two separate sets of books.
  • A dead hard drive takes it with it.
So: Settings → Download backup, once a week. Save the file to Google Drive or email it to yourself. If anything ever goes wrong, Restore from backup reads that same file straight back in.

There is a proper fix for this — see below.
