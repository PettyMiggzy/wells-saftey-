Common Questions

The things carriers and brokers ask us most, answered plainly. Your permit is always the final word — but this is how it usually works.

Do I need a pilot car for my load?

Your permit is the authority — every state writes escort requirements into the permit itself, based on the load's width, height, length and sometimes weight, and the requirement often changes with the type of road. Send us the dimensions and the route and we will read the requirement back to you before you book anything. If your load does not need an escort, we will tell you that too.

What is the difference between a lead car and a chase car?

A lead car runs ahead of the load: it warns oncoming traffic, calls out hazards, holds intersections and spots problems while there is still room to react. A chase car runs behind: it shields the load from traffic coming up fast, manages lane changes and covers the rear during turns and backing. Wide loads on two-lane roads usually need a lead; loads on multi-lane highways more often need a chase. Plenty of permits call for both.

What does a pilot car cost?

Escort work is normally billed per loaded mile, with a day rate or minimum so a short run is still worth dispatching. On top of that: deadhead miles to get to you, wait or detention time if you are held up, and hotel or per diem on overnight runs. High pole and steer car work carry a higher mile rate than a plain lead or chase. Send the dimensions and the lane and you get a firm number, usually the same day.

What is a height pole car and when do I need one?

A high pole car runs ahead of an over-height load with a pole standing upright, set a few inches above the measured load height. It rides straight the whole way. If it touches something, it bends — and that is the point: the pole takes the hit so your load does not, and the move stops before the trailer is anywhere near it. Most over-height permits require one.

How far in advance should I book an escort?

As soon as you have the permit or a firm pickup date. Long multi-state runs and superloads want the most notice because they involve the most coordination. That said, dispatch is answered around the clock and short notice is worth a call — sometimes we can cover it.

What states do you cover?

All 48 contiguous states. We are based in Martinsville, Indiana, sitting on the I-70, I-65 and I-69 corridors, and we run coast to coast. On long runs we keep the same driver with the load end to end wherever we can, so you have one point of contact and one invoice instead of a handoff at every state line.

Are you certified and insured?

Yes. Our operators hold PEVO certification along with ATSSA flagger, WITPAC and TIMS incident-management training. We carry commercial auto and general liability, and we will send a certificate naming your company as certificate holder — plus copies of the credential cards — before the move.

What happens if the load will not clear something on the route?

That is exactly what the pole and the route survey are for: the problem gets found before your load is under it. When we hit one, the load stops, and we work the reroute with you and the permit office. Finding it early costs you an hour; finding it late costs you a bridge strike.

Do you handle permits?

We are not a permit service, but we work to your permit and we know how to read them. Send yours over and we will confirm what escorts it actually requires, so you are not paying for a car you do not need or arriving one short.

Can you run at night or on weekends?

Yes, where the permit allows it. Many oversize permits restrict movement to daylight, or to specific windows, and some loads are required to move at night to keep traffic clear. We run permitted night windows with lighting to match, and dispatch is staffed 24/7 — including weekends and holidays.

What is a superload?

A load big or heavy enough that it goes beyond routine oversize permitting — the threshold varies by state, and it usually brings extra requirements: multiple escorts, police escort, utility crews to lift lines, bridge analysis, or a restricted movement window. We put together and run the whole escort package for those, and coordinate with everyone else on the move.

How do I get a quote?

Call or text dispatch on (765) 684-1909, or send the details through the quote form. What we need: dimensions (width, height, length, weight), origin and destination, the move date, and the permit if you have it. You get a firm quote back, usually the same day.
